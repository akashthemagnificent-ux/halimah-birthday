# assets/sky — two nights the sky was watching

Real NASA imagery the owner downloaded for the birthday site's `#starmap`
section (redesigned as "two nights the sky was watching"). Both files came from
the owner's Windows Downloads folder and were optimized here to WebP for the web
(the originals stay on the owner's machine).

| file | source | object | night |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `night-born-rosette.webp` | "the night that she was born.jpg" | the Rosette Nebula (NGC 2237), a stellar nursery | 19 Oct 2011, the night she was born |
| `night-together-ring.webp` | "On the night we got together.png" | the Ring Nebula (M57) as imaged by the James Webb Space Telescope | 26 Aug 2026, the night we got together |

## What each image is, and why it is here

- **Rosette Nebula (born night).** A rose-shaped cloud of hydrogen roughly
  5,000 light-years away, in the constellation Monoceros. It is a stellar
  nursery: new stars are being born inside it right now. Placed against the
  night she was born because it is, literally, a place where stars are made.
- **Ring Nebula / JWST (together night).** A dying sun-like star has shed its
  outer layers into a glowing ring; the Webb telescope resolves the fine shells
  and the faint galaxies scattered behind it. Placed against the night we got
  together because it looks like a ring, and because that is the same night as
  the "YES A MILLION TIMES YES" chat.

## Provenance / reuse notes

- NASA/ESA imagery is credited in the section copy ("as photographed by
  NASA / the James Webb Space Telescope"), never stripped.
- No coordinates, city, latitude, or constellation *data* is derived from these
  files. They are decorative photographic plates; the site draws no astronomy
  from them (the audit forbids invented geography).
- Keep the aspect ratio square. The section frames them as square plates.
